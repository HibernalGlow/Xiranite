# Legal Jurisdiction Motion Identity

## Style Prompt

Create a light, institutional Chinese legal explainer that feels like a precise case-routing system rather than a slideshow. Use large keywords, court and document pictograms, visible paths, boundaries, gates, and state changes. The viewer should understand containment, exclusion, and prerequisites through motion before reading the labels. Keep the frame editorial and work-focused, with restrained paper texture, strong rules, and asymmetric compositions.

## Colors

- Canvas: `#F3F5F2`
- Paper: `#FFFFFF`
- Ink: `#17201D`
- Muted ink: `#66716C`
- Rule: `#CBD2CE`
- Court red: `#C83F35`
- Mediation gold: `#A8791D`
- Labor teal: `#087C73`
- Scope blue: `#3768A5`

## Typography

- Chinese display and labels: `Microsoft YaHei`, weights 700-900
- Metadata and scene numbers: `Consolas`, weight 700
- Headlines are at least 60px; supporting labels are at least 20px.

## Motion

- Dominant current: RIGHT.
- Primary seam: cut-the-curve on the x axis.
- One Z-forward seam enters the organization-relationship chapter.
- Every action must add information: staged reveal, routed token, boundary change, stamp impact, barrier drop, or checkpoint release.
- Hold 0.45 seconds after the judicial-confirmation stamp before revealing enforceability.

## What NOT to Do

- Do not reproduce a table or show paragraph-length legal text.
- Do not crossfade between scenes or leave a scene empty before a cut.
- Do not use idle floating, breathing, pulsing, or decorative orbs.
- Do not use gradients, oversized rounded cards, or a single-hue palette.
- Do not animate every element with the same direction, duration, or easing.
